\begin{abstract}
Intermittent frame hitches are among the most visible performance defects in
games and among the hardest to diagnose: they are rare, and their cause often
lies outside the game, in the scheduler, the memory manager, storage or
another process. Existing tools either keep raw events around each slow
frame and leave the diagnosis to an engineer, or judge stalls one at a time
with no notion of the frame they belong to. This paper presents \sys, an
eBPF tool that explains each over-budget frame of an unmodified Linux game
when the frame ends. \sys partitions the presenting thread's frame by kernel
cause, groups its waits by who ended them, and follows each group through a
chain of wakeups carried forward in task-local storage, separating time
inherited from a stalled waker from time spent waiting for one that was
running. Records are built in the kernel and emitted only for frames over
budget. On a benchmark of \hbnClasses\ injected stall classes, \sys recorded
exactly the \hbtotOver\ over-budget frames among \hbtotFrames; in ten case
studies that reproduce documented Linux gaming stutters, its records
explained nearly every affected frame. On rare hitches it was the only one
of seven tools to attribute nearly all of them and name their culprit, at
155 to 900 times less output than the raw recorders that also held them,
for a per-switch cost in the range of existing scheduler tracers.

\end{abstract}
